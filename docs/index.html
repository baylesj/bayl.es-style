theme

How the *.bayl.es pages look. The colors and type are the ones the sites already use; this page is set in them. Every site opens on the same galaxy, seen almost edge-on, with its own waypoint lit. Where a site has something to measure, its key says what.

01principles

02signatures

Every site shares one galaxy: a plane seen almost edge-on and running off the page, six range rings, a faint radial grid, and the same stars from one fixed seed. Each site is a waypoint on a stalk to the plane; the one you are on is lit in its star color and bracketed, the rest stay dim. The site's full hue marks the prompt path, labels, panels, the selection bracket and the primary button.

03keys

Steps measure what a site is for, never the server. Where the measure has a direction, the key diverges: green on the side that's fine, amber on the side that wants a look, comment grey in the middle. Green is already the OK role and amber the needs-attention role, so neither changes meaning. Orange stays for down and failed and never appears on a key. A step is a filled or outlined square, and always has its word.

05colors

The current tokens, unchanged. Each ratio is measured live in this theme and site, against the page and against the panel tint; the lower one decides. Text needs 4.5:1, marks 3:1, and the galaxy's rings and stars are decoration with no text on them.

06type

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for words and its mono for machine facts: one family, both kept at reading sizes. Atkinson was drawn so letters that look alike stay apart (Il1, O0), which matters when most text is small. No display sizes and no tight tracking: the largest thing on a page is a 22px wordmark, and it ends without a period.

08contrast

Every text color meets 4.5:1 on the page and on the panel tint, in both themes, on every site; marks meet 3:1. That includes button labels on each site's fill, measured here:

jordan and orbit use --pink-text for text and the button, since white on plain pink is 3.8:1. Unavailable means grey and a reason, never faded text.

09favicons

Seven stars. Each favicon is the Big Dipper on a night tile with the site's own star lit in its star color and the tile edged in its hue, and the header's waypoints are the same seven stars. The server, eopsin, is named for 업신 (Eopsin), the Korean household goddess of storage and wealth, usually imagined as a rat snake.1 On Jeju the wealth goddess is Chilseongsin, almost exactly the same as the mainland Eopsin,1 and chilseong (七星) means seven stars: the Big Dipper. The first seven bayl.es sites are its stars.

Dubhe is home, Merak my, Phecda id, Megrez audible, Alioth orbit, Mizar zero and Alkaid jordan; Alcor, beside Mizar, is climb. Home Assistant keeps HA's own blue and carries the server's mark instead of a star: 업 in Sunflower, in ink.

An eighth site takes Alcor, the faint companion beside Mizar in the Dipper's handle, long used as a test of eyesight.2 In Chinese astronomy Alcor is 輔, the Assistant.3 Taoist tradition counts the Dipper as nine: the seven stars and two not visible to the naked eye,4 so a ninth site takes the other unseen one. Past nine, a site is a field star in the galaxy, not part of the Dipper.

10influences

The header is after the Sensors Manager in Homeworld (Relic Entertainment, 1999), the view of the entire game map with every visible ship.5 From it come the plane seen almost edge-on, its range rings, contacts on stalks to the plane and the corner brackets around what is selected.

Light mode is after the Guidestone, the stone map in the same game that marks the way home:5 a blue-grey slate with the galaxy etched on it in pale lines. The slate is mixed from the palette, comment grey with a little of the purple role, so it adds no new hue.

The lit stars are each site's hue mixed mostly with starlight, so they read as stars rather than as brand colors. Nothing here is taken from the game: no art, no logos, no fonts. The name eopsin and the seven stars are under favicons.

11never

  • a hero: no display-size wordmark, no paragraph selling the system
  • card grids, pill buttons, glossy or shadowed controls
  • blends in the keys; steps are hard
  • a key that measures the server on a site that serves people
  • a color without its word
  • orange on a key; it means down or failed
  • words over the plane or the stars; the readout sits on its own patch of ground
  • a contact without its word; color and height are never the only sign
  • motion in the galaxy; it holds still, like a paused map
  • new hues; derive from the tokens
  • a period after the wordmark, the name or the "e"; a colored dot reads as a brand mark
  • cream or beige grounds, emoji, Inter, Roboto, Arial, Geist
